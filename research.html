Improving Field Emission Device Performance via Finite Element Analysis

Co-authored with Jaden Lu (primary author)

Cold cathode field emitters offer high current density but fail quickly as Joule heating deforms the emitter tip. This project modeled emitter geometry, spacing, and doping in COMSOL to identify configurations that stay stable under heavy current — trading off electric field strength against heat dissipation to extend device lifetime.

Climate-Driven Temporal Analysis of Global Agricultural Trade Networks

Primary author, with Arun K. Rajasekaran

Built a directed, weighted network model of global trade in beans, wheat, and potatoes using 37 years of FAO data, then tested four forecasting approaches (Auto ARIMA, linear and polynomial regression, Gaussian processes) to project trade volumes through 2040. Comparing 2006 against a projected 2038 revealed trade networks shifting from fragmented to far more interconnected — evidence that climate pressure is pushing countries toward strategic trade alliances rather than isolation.

The Role of Private Funding for Public Goods in a Competitive Market

Sole author

A comparative case-study analysis across Africa, Europe, and the U.S. arguing that market-driven funding — philanthropy, subscriptions, advertising — sustains diverse, high-quality journalism more effectively than tax-based funding alone, by rewarding outlets that adapt fastest to audience needs while still leaving room for limited public support where access gaps remain.

2025 — IISES Conference on Economics, Finance & Business, London

Assessing the Effectiveness of Bilateral Aid on Financial Access

Sole author

A decade-long comparison of Bangladesh, Rwanda, and Mozambique testing whether foreign aid actually expands financial inclusion or just injects short-term cash. Finding: aid works when it aligns with a government’s existing priorities — Bangladesh and Rwanda kept growing microfinance and digital banking even as aid tapered off, while Mozambique’s financial access stalled the moment donor support faded, pointing to institutional strength as the real deciding factor.

2025 — 3rd World Conference on Management, Business & Finance, London

Quantifying the Effects of Human Capital on Governance

Sole author

A multivariate regression across Rwanda, Ethiopia, South Sudan, and the Central African Republic (2000–2022) isolating education spending as the strongest predictor of governance strength (CPIA scores) — ahead of healthcare and social protection. Used the model to project three future scenarios and built a short-, medium-, and long-term policy roadmap for fragile states based on where Rwanda’s investment path diverged from South Sudan’s.

2025 — Proc. SPIE 13360, Physics & Simulation of Optoelectronic Devices XXXIII

Finite Element Modeling of InAs/GaSb/AlSb Superlattice Photosensors

Primary author, with Jaden Lu

Infrared sensors need materials tuned precisely to a target wavelength. This project used COMSOL to simulate different quantum-well structures in the InAs/GaSb/AlSb material family, comparing how well-thickness, doping, and layer count each shift detection range and current output — mapping design levers an actual fabrication team could use to hit a target infrared band.

Primary author — modeling architecture & analysisSuperlattice modeling
2025 — IEEE ITherm, Dallas

Finite Element Analysis of Porous Silicon-Based Thermoelectric Devices

Primary author, with Jaden Lu

Silicon is cheap and well-understood but a poor thermoelectric material on its own. This project modeled how controlled porosity changes silicon’s thermal and electrical conductivity — and, critically, how it holds up mechanically under strain and temperature swings — to map a practical path toward silicon-based thermoelectric generators that don’t fail under real operating conditions.

Filed Sept. 2025 — USPTO Application No. 63/888,059

Hybrid Photovoltaic–Thermoelectric (PV–TEG) Generator, U.S. Provisional Patent

Co-inventor, with Jaden Lu

Solar panels lose efficiency as they heat up. This invention captures that wasted heat with a thermoelectric layer built from horizontally arrayed p-n junctions — a departure from conventional vertical designs — simultaneously cooling the solar cell and generating extra power. Simulation and prototype testing showed an 8–12% efficiency gain and triple the voltage output of standard thermoelectric devices, with applications spanning renewable energy, automotive waste-heat recovery, and biomedical micro-power systems.
