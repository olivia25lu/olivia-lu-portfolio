Two Android apps, each one starting from a specific, unglamorous problem rather than a technology I wanted to try out.

Android App
CivicPulse
2024

Reporting a pothole shouldn’t require knowing who to call

CivicPulse came out of a genuine gap I saw sitting on the Mayor’s Youth Commission: residents rarely knew the right channel to report something like a broken traffic light or a pothole, and city departments received reports with no location, no urgency level, and no photo to triage from. CivicPulse lets a citizen identify a problem and submit a structured service request in under a minute — photo, GPS location, issue type, and urgency level all attached automatically, along with the user’s contact details for follow-up.

Java · Android Studio

Android App
AlgebraXpert
2023

90% of a school survey called math their hardest subject — so I built something adaptive

AlgebraXpert started with a survey I ran at school that found nine in ten students considered math their hardest subject, usually because practice problems didn’t adjust to where they were actually struggling. I built an adaptive math-practice app on Android Studio using Java, designed to meet students at their actual skill level rather than pushing everyone through the same problem set.

The distribution mattered as much as the build: through Hope in the Desert’s partnership with Arizona Students Recycling Used Technology, AlgebraXpert went out on refurbished devices to students who might not otherwise have had a personal device for practice at all. Within a month of use, 96% of surveyed students reported improved math confidence and skills.
